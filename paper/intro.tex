This study asks three questions, fixed at design time rather than derived
post hoc from whatever pattern the data happens to show:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (main effect).} Holding the narrative content of a
    relationship-norm-violation vignette fixed, does an LLM's fault
    rating for the norm-violating agent (\texttt{fault\_rating}, Section
    \ref{sec:protocol}) differ systematically between the male-agent and
    female-agent gender configurations?
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (heterogeneity).} Does any such effect vary by norm
    family (e.g.\ household/emotional-labor families vs.\ jealousy or
    sexuality-and-intimacy families), by severity level, or across the
    five evaluated models/four providers?
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (hedging as a signal, not noise).} Does the rate at
    which a model hedges, refuses to commit, or fails schema validation
    itself vary by agent gender or norm family -- i.e.\ is differential
    willingness to render a judgment, not just the judgment's value, a
    site of bias?
\end{itemize}
