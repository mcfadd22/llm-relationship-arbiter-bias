# Unverified Sources

These sources were cited at some point in the project's design conversations but have **not been independently confirmed** against a primary source or bibliographic database in this session's audit. They are not known to be fabricated — they were carried over from an earlier conversation's citation, given from memory rather than freshly checked — but their exact title wording, page/chapter numbers, and publication details should be treated as unconfirmed until verified directly. Do not cite these in any formal write-up (paper, grant report, etc.) without checking them first.

---

**Brake, Elizabeth (2010).** "Willing Parents: A Voluntarist Account of Parental Role Obligations." In *Procreation and Parenthood*, D. Archard & D. Benatar (eds.), Oxford University Press.

Cited in the main lit review as a contemporary counterpoint to Hardimon (1994), arguing role obligations should be voluntarily entered into for them to genuinely bind. Also referenced alongside a claimed 2012 book, *Minimizing Marriage* (Oxford University Press), as a more recent (~last 10 years) source if that mattered for the obligation-taxonomy write-up.

**What's plausible:** Elizabeth Brake is a real philosopher who publishes on marriage, parental obligation, and voluntarist accounts of role-based duties — this is consistent with her known body of work. The general claim (a voluntarist counterpoint to Hardimon) is a real position in that literature.

**What's unconfirmed:** the exact chapter title, the specific edited volume title and editors (*Procreation and Parenthood*, Archard & Benatar), the publisher, and the year. The *Minimizing Marriage* citation was also not independently checked.

---

**Goodin, Robert E. (1985).** *Protecting the Vulnerable: A Reanalysis of Our Social Responsibilities.* University of Chicago Press.

Cited in the main lit review as a non-care-ethics (liberal/consequentialist) counterpoint to Noddings/Held/Kittay, arguing that vulnerability itself generates obligation independent of a care-ethics framework.

**What's plausible:** Robert E. Goodin is a real political philosopher who has written extensively on vulnerability, responsibility, and protective obligation — this is consistent with his known work, and *Protecting the Vulnerable* is a real, well-known title associated with him.

**What's unconfirmed:** the exact subtitle, publisher, and year were not independently re-checked against a primary bibliographic source in this session's audit, even though they are very likely correct.

---

## What to do with these

Before using either source in anything that needs to be precise (a citation list, a formal literature review, a grant or paper submission):
1. Search for the exact title + author + year directly.
2. Confirm publisher, year, and (if citing a specific claim) page numbers.
3. If confirmed, move the entry back into the main lit review (`lit_review.md`) with a note that it's now verified.
4. If a source turns out to be wrong or unfindable, treat it the way the earlier "Contarino, S." error was handled in the main lit review — flag it explicitly as a correction rather than quietly removing it, so there's a record of what changed and why.
